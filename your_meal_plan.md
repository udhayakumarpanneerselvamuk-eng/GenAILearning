Reusable Prompt: 7-Day South Indian Vegetarian Weight-Loss Meal Planner
R – Role

Act as an experienced nutritionist and South Indian vegetarian meal-planning expert. You have strong knowledge of balanced nutrition, portion control, traditional South Indian cuisine and sustainable weight management.

T – Task

Create a healthy, balanced and practical 7-day South Indian vegetarian meal plan for an individual who wants to lose weight gradually and sustainably.

The plan must cover Monday to Sunday and include:

Breakfast
Mid-morning snack
Lunch
Evening snack
Dinner
C – Context

Follow these guidelines when creating the meal plan:

Use traditional South Indian vegetarian foods such as idli, dosa, sambar, rasam, pongal, upma, vegetable kurma, kootu, poriyal and millet dishes.
Include protein-rich foods such as lentils, dal, chickpeas, green gram, beans, paneer, tofu and curd.
Include sufficient vegetables, fruits and fibre.
Use moderate portions of rice, millets and other carbohydrates.
Prefer steaming, boiling, grilling and cooking with moderate amounts of oil.
Minimise deep-fried foods, added sugar, highly processed foods and excessive oil.
Include affordable ingredients that are easily available in a typical Indian grocery store.
Ensure meals are varied, nutritious, filling and easy to prepare.
Avoid crash diets, meal skipping and extreme calorie restrictions.
Do not assume that one calorie target is suitable for everyone.

Use the following personal information as customisable inputs:

Age: [27]
Gender: [Male]

Current weight: [94]
Target weight: [70]
Activity level: [Sedentary]
Food allergies or restrictions: [No Restriction avoid beef, pork, fish]
Health conditions: [No]

If essential personal information is missing, ask for it before calculating individual calorie or protein targets. Do not invent personal health information.

F – Few-shot examples

Follow these examples when selecting meals:

Example 1: Breakfast: 2 idlis with one bowl of vegetable sambar.

Example 2: Lunch: A moderate portion of rice, keerai kootu, vegetable poriyal and plain curd.

Example 3: Evening snack: One fruit with a small handful of roasted chana.

Example 4: Dinner: 2 small dosa with sambar and vegetable chutney.

Use these examples as guidance, not as meals that must be repeated throughout the week. Create different combinations for all seven days.

R – Response Format

Present the final answer in the following structure:

1. Personalised Nutrition Overview
Briefly explain the weight-loss approach.
Provide an appropriate estimated daily calorie and protein range, if sufficient personal information is available.
Explain portion control.
2. Seven-Day Meal Plan

Create a table with these columns:

Day	Breakfast	Mid-morning Snack	Lunch	Evening Snack	Dinner

Include suggested portion sizes for every meal.

3. Daily Nutritional Breakdown

For each day, provide:

Approximate total calories.
Approximate protein.
A brief nutritional observation.

Clearly label nutritional values as estimates.

4. Weekly Grocery Shopping List

Organise the ingredients into:

Vegetables
Fruits
Grains and millets
Pulses and legumes
Dairy and protein sources
Nuts, seeds and other essentials
5. Healthy Cooking and Weight-Loss Tips

Provide 5–7 practical tips covering portion control, hydration, meal preparation, physical activity and consistency.

6. Final Recommendation

Summarise how to follow the meal plan sustainably and how to make suitable food substitutions.

IMPORTANT: Keep the meal plan practical, affordable, culturally appropriate, nutritionally balanced and easy to follow. Do not promise rapid weight loss or prescribe an unsafe calorie deficit. Recommend professional dietary advice where medical conditions or special nutritional needs are involved.
